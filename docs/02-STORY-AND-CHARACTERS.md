# 02 — Story and characters

## Story premise
A young family member returns to a Bihar village as Chhath approaches. Their mother is observing the fast in this family's tradition. The player notices that the festival's visible beauty depends on many quiet acts: preparing the home, tending materials, helping neighbours, making offerings and caring for one another.

## Emotional arc
Arrival and uncertainty → learning the work → growing responsibility → shared effort → quiet night → first light → offering and sharing.

## Character roles
- **Player:** curious, helpful, learns by doing. Avoid making them a perfect expert.
- **Mother / Vrati:** thoughtful and determined, with agency, preferences and boundaries. She can delegate, rest and ask for support.
- **Grandmother / elder:** remembers local practice and explains why a task matters. Her words are not automatically universal rules.
- **Father / uncle / other family member:** participates in preparation, logistics and carrying; not a background bystander.
- **Neighbour:** represents mutual aid and reciprocal community.
- **Bamboo artisan / vendor:** demonstrates craft and the local economy.
- **Community organiser:** coordinates lane and ghat readiness.

## Dialogue rules
- Write short, natural lines.
- Provide English and Hindi UI text; Bhojpuri lines only after native-speaker review.
- Do not fake an accent by misspelling words.
- Use optional subtitles and a language toggle.
- Avoid exposition dumps. Let props, tasks and conversations tell the story.

## Sample placeholder dialogue (not final cultural testimony)
Player: “What should I help with first?”
Elder: “Look around the courtyard. We will decide together what needs attention.”
Mother: “Please finish your part carefully. There is no need to rush.”
Neighbour: “We have cleaned our side. Can you help carry these things to the lane?”

These are narrative placeholders, not claims about exact ritual wording.

## Story beats
- Beat 1: homecoming and the courtyard.
- Beat 2: learning preparation through practical work.
- Beat 3: meeting neighbours and artisans.
- Beat 4: household and village preparation converge.
- Beat 5: the quiet night before dawn.
- Beat 6: walk to the ghat, Usha Arghya, first light.
- Beat 7: prasad shared; a calm closing image.
